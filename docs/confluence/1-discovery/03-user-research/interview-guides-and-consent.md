> **ℹ️ About this page.** The consent script and the interview guides for the firefighters, duty chiefs and commander of a *Corpo de Bombeiros Voluntários*, used to understand how the *piquete* is composed today (Assignment §5.3 interviews; §11 informed participants). The interviewer and the note-taker read it before every session; answers go into one research note per session, created from **Research Note — Template**, never into this page.

## Principles

- Ask about past behaviour, not a hypothetical future: "the last time…", never "would you…".
- Don't pitch the product: do not describe Escala or its features during the interview.
- About 20 minutes; stop on time.
- One interviewer and one note-taker; the note-taker marks a quote as verbatim only when those exact words were said.

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** After each answer, ask "What happened next?" or "Why?" before moving on. Never complete an answer the participant did not give; write "not asked" or "no answer".

> **✏️ Example (illustrative — replace).** Bad: "Would you use an app that checks whether someone's TAS is valid?" (hypothetical, and it pitches the product). Good: "How do you know someone's TAS is valid?", then "Where did you look it up the last time?"

## Consent script (English)

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** Read the script word for word before the first question and record the answer as "Consent confirmed (Y/N)" in the research note. If the answer is no, thank the person and take no notes. The script promises no names and no recording; the notes must keep that promise.

> "This is an academic project at ISEP. We are not evaluating anyone or reporting anything to the command. We take notes without names to understand how the *piquete* is composed. We don't record. You can stop at any time and ask us to delete what you said. Shall we continue?"

## Consent script (Portuguese — read aloud)

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** Read this version aloud, word for word, to Portuguese-speaking participants. It must say the same as the English version: if one changes, change both.

> "Isto é um projeto académico do ISEP. Não estamos a avaliar ninguém nem a reportar nada ao comando. Tomamos notas sem nomes, para perceber como funciona a composição do piquete. Não gravamos. Pode parar quando quiser e pedir que apaguemos o que disse. Podemos continuar?"

## Guide — Firefighter

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** Q2 and Q5 test A1; Q3 tests A2; Q4 tests A6; Q1 tests A3 indirectly (which channels people already use), never by asking about an app. Q2 also shows how people phrase an unavailability: note the patterns (e.g. abbreviations) for the team-written dataset on **06 · Responsible AI Opportunity Assessment**, never real messages.

1. How did you learn about your last *piquete*? How much notice did you get?
2. Tell me about the last time you could not go. What did you do?
3. Were you ever asked to crew a vehicle you were not qualified for?
4. Do you know how many hours you still owe this year? How do you know?
5. When a colleague drops out, how is it solved?

## Guide — Duty chief

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** Q1 and Q2 test A1 (Q2 also shows whether extra duty always falls on the same people); Q3 and Q5 test A2; Q4 tests A4, the riskiest assumption: if it is false, the IPMA integration must change. When they say they look something up, ask where, and ask whether we may observe a composition (**Observation Grid**).

1. Walk me through the last *piquete* composition, from start to end.
2. In what order do you contact people? Why that order?
3. How do you know someone's TAS is valid?
4. What changes on a maximum fire-risk day?
5. Did you ever assign someone and later realise you shouldn't have? What happened?

## Guide — Commander

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** Q1 tests A6; Q2 tests A5 and feeds RN07; Q3 tests A4 and A5 and feeds RN10 (both "to obtain" on **Requirements and Quality Attributes**); Q4 shows which records matter for audit and statutory hours.

1. How do you follow the statutory hours of the active roster?
2. What does the internal regulation say about rest between duty periods?
3. Who defines the required composition per risk level?
4. What would you have to show in an inspection?

## Closing questions (all)

> **ℹ️ Guidance.** Ask all three at the end of every interview. Record referrals by role (e.g. "another duty chief"), never by name; they get a code only when they take part. Answers to the second question count as "conversation with a user" evidence on **02 · Market and Competitor Analysis**.

1. "Is there anyone else we should talk to?"
2. "Do you use Ifprotec, GESCORP or other software? What doesn't it solve for you?"
3. "Could we see the internal regulation?"
